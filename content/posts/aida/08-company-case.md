## Summary

Engestofte Gods presents concrete challenges from its business. The task is to explore where AI could offer value—or where conventional software and agent-assisted development might be more appropriate—and turn one promising direction into a project proposal.

Real projects begin with incomplete, contextual problems rather than a ready-made technical specification. Speaking with a company helps connect technical possibilities to users, workflows, constraints, and value. It also prevents the team from choosing AI simply because the course is about AI.

## From presentation to proposal

The company visit provides the starting point for project discovery. A useful idea proposal should capture:

- The concrete business problem or opportunity described by Engestofte Gods.
- The person or role that experiences the problem in daily work.
- The information, documents, or systems a possible solution would depend on.
- Where an AI capability could contribute and where normal application logic would be enough.
- The expected value for the company and a small prototype that could demonstrate it.
- Important assumptions, risks, and questions that still require input from the company.

Code agents may help the team develop the solution even if the final product itself does not require an AI feature. This distinction keeps the project open to the technology that best fits the case.

## Key takeaways

The discovery process should begin with the problem and the people affected by it. A useful proposal identifies the user, current difficulty, expected value, available data, risks, and a small outcome that can be tested. The choice of AI should follow from those facts.

- Listen for workflow details rather than only feature requests.
- Separate confirmed facts from assumptions made by the project team.
- Prefer a narrow, testable case over a broad promise to “automate” the business.
- Define how the company could recognize whether the prototype is useful.

## Closing note

The temptation in an AI project is to start with a model and search for a use case. This session points in the opposite direction: understand the organization first, then select the simplest technology that genuinely improves its situation.
